## Course Description and Requisites

## Course Goals

### Topics

The objective of the topics covered in the course is to make students aware of the importance of technical communication to their future profession.

### Basic Communication Skills

Introduces basic principles of good communication, demonstrated through different forms of oral and written communication:

- Establishing a common frame of reference
- Establishing the objective of the communication
- Simplicity
- Conciseness
- Feedback

### Oral Communication

Students will practice the basic principles of communication through oral presentations:

- Identifying the needs of the audience
- Defining the objective of the communication
- Scaling the amount of data to the time available
- Strategies for providing backup material
- Ensuring the message was understood

### Written Communication

Students will use material and information relating to current research topics in Computer Engineering as subject matter for different forms of written communication, which may include:

- Technical Description / technical proposal
- Memos, Project Proposal, Progress Report
- Lab Reports, Research Report
- Trip / Conference Report

### Documentation

Students will learn proper professional standards for documentation including IEEE-style references, citation, and formatting.

## Course Learning Outcomes (CLOs)

1. Analyze and critique complex ideas, arguments, and methodologies presented in scholarly and technical discourse.
2. Locate, evaluate, and effectively use advanced library resources, electronic databases, and discipline-specific tools to conduct research.
3. Organize, synthesize, and interpret information from diverse scholarly and technical sources to produce a coherent, graduate-level literature review.
4. Distinguish and evaluate scholarly versus non-scholarly sources, and articulate the characteristics of rigorous scholarly writing related to content, structure, and style.
5. Compose, revise, and edit discipline-specific documents, such as technical reports and research papers, using appropriate editorial conventions and citation standards (e.g., IEEE, ACM).
6. Design and integrate effective visual and multimodal communication elements (e.g., figures, tables, diagrams) to enhance clarity and persuasiveness in technical writing.
7. Adapt writing style, organization, and rhetorical strategies to address the expectations of varied audiences.
8. Apply principles of academic integrity, recognizing and preventing plagiarism through ethical use, paraphrasing, and citation of sources.
9. Plan and deliver clear, well-organized oral presentations using professional communication strategies and supporting materials suitable for technical audiences.

## Course Requirements and Assignments

### Course Requirements

SJSU classes are designed such that, to be successful, students are expected to spend a minimum of **45 hours per unit of credit** (normally 3 hours per unit per week), including preparing for class, participating in course activities, completing assignments, and so on. More details on student workload can be found in [University Policy S12-3](http://www.sjsu.edu/senate/docs/S12-3.pdf).

> [!important] Academic Integrity Please read the sections on University Policies and academic integrity carefully. Because this class requires much research and writing, students are often tempted to copy ideas and sentences from sources on the Internet, published research papers, or each other's papers.
> 
> The use of AI (ChatGPT, Grammarly, or any other chatbot or AI tool) is **not recommended** in this class, unless specifically included in the assignment requirements. Violation of the academic integrity policy will result in the student being reported for breach of academic integrity, a zero on the assignment, and — in serious cases — a failing grade for the course.
> 
> - Acceptable Turnitin score: **≤ 20%**
> - Acceptable AI use detection score: **≤ 50%**

### Assignment Submission Policies

- Only **1 attempt** is provided for the submission of the majority of course assignments. Submit only final, polished work — this avoids reliance on Turnitin scores as a plagiarism check.
- **Late Assignment Policy:** 10% penalty if prior arrangement with the instructor is not made before the deadline. No assignments accepted more than one week after the deadline — submissions remain open for only one week after the official deadline.
- **In-class worksheet policy:** Submit by the end of class on the day assigned. Worksheets completed outside of class will not be accepted, and there are no make-ups for missed in-class assignments. Students who miss the preceding lecture receive a 0 on the worksheet even if submitted.
- Unless explicitly specified, assignments are to be completed **individually**, without collaboration with peers.
- All homework and in-class writing must be submitted to **Canvas**, not email.
- **Participation points:** Worth 10% of the course grade, scored on completion of in-class worksheets and discussion posts.
